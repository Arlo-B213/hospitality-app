# THE SERVICE STACK™
### A Complete Hospitality Leadership & Soft Skills Development System
*Building Leaders and Culture, One Shift at a Time*

Preston Lee's philosophy: build the system once, make it repeatable, then trust your people to run it. This isn't a binder of feel-good posters — it's an operating system for how leaders think under pressure and how the front line treats every guest, built so any shift lead can pick it up and run it without a manager standing over their shoulder.

---

## 1. PROGRAM OVERVIEW & MANAGEMENT PILLARS

**The Big Idea:** Culture doesn't happen because a poster went up in the break room. It happens because leaders have five specific muscles trained, and those muscles get exercised on the floor, every shift, in real moments — a call-out at 11:45am, a wrong ticket at the window, a guest who's clearly had a rough day. The Service Stack trains those five muscles first, because a manager who has them can teach anything downstream. A manager who doesn't will burn through soft-skills training with nothing to hang it on.

### The 5 Pillars (Critical Management Strengths)

**Pillar 1 — Emotional Intelligence (Reading the Room)**
The ability to clock the guest who's about to complain before they say a word, and to notice the cashier who's five minutes from tears before it shows up in their tone. On a high-volume floor, EQ isn't soft — it's the early warning system. A leader with this pillar catches problems while they're still cheap to fix.

**Pillar 2 — Coaching & Developing Others**
Anyone can hand someone a training packet. This pillar is about turning a mediocre appraisal conversation into a real skill-building moment — catching someone doing something right *in the moment* and naming it, not just writing it down six months later. This is where "training" stops being a checkbox and becomes actual growth.

**Pillar 3 — Decisiveness Under Pressure**
The fryer's down, three items just got 86'd, and the POS is frozen with a line to the door. This pillar is the ability to make a call — the *right-enough* call, fast — and own it. Indecision in the weeds is contagious; it spreads panic down the line faster than any single mistake would.

**Pillar 4 — Delegation & Accountability**
Trusting a lead to run an outlet without hovering, while still holding them to the numbers and the standard. Delegation without accountability is abandonment. Accountability without delegation is micromanagement. This pillar is the discipline of doing both at once.

**Pillar 5 — Cross-Functional Communication**
Translating what's actually happening on the floor into language upper management can act on — and translating strategy from upstairs into something a 19-year-old cashier can actually execute during a Saturday rush. Leaders who are weak here become bottlenecks in both directions.

## 2. SERVICE ENVIRONMENT WORKFLOW MATRIX

Same 4 modules, three completely different floors. A choreography move in fine dining would get someone run over in a food court. This matrix is how a manager transferring between outlet types — or training leads across concepts — knows exactly what "good" looks like in each environment.

### Module 1: THE GUEST READ *(Emotional Intelligence · Empathy · Active Listening)*

| Tier | What "Reading the Room" Looks Like | How It's Taught |
|---|---|---|
| **QSR / Food Court** | Scanning the line for the guest who's getting impatient, the one juggling kids and a tray, the one who looks lost reading the menu board. Reads happen in 2-3 seconds, at volume, without slowing the line down. | Speed-drill role plays: cashier gets a flashcard guest type, has 5 seconds to identify the read and adjust their opener before the "guest" reaches the register. |
| **Casual Dining** | Reading a table's pace — are they rushing to catch a show, settling in for a long lunch, celebrating something? Reading body language across a whole section, not just one interaction. | Table-read shadowing: new server shadows a lead for one shift, calls out what they think each table's "vibe" is, lead confirms or corrects in real time. |
| **Fine Dining** | Anticipating an unspoken need before the guest raises a hand — noticing the empty water glass, the couple who's clearly on a first date and wants privacy, the regular whose order just changed. | Silent-service drills: server must identify and act on 3 unspoken cues during a single mock course without being told what to look for. |

### Module 2: THE RECOVERY PLAY *(Decisiveness Under Pressure · Stress Tolerance · Adaptability)*

| Tier | What "Recovery" Looks Like | How It's Taught |
|---|---|---|
| **QSR / Food Court** | Item's 86'd mid-rush — cashier redirects the guest to an alternative in one breath, no hesitation, no apology spiral that backs up the line. | "86 Drills": trainer randomly calls out an 86'd item mid-shift (low-stakes hours), cashier has 3 seconds to pivot the guest before the next person in line notices. |
| **Casual Dining** | Kitchen's backed up, a table's ticket time is creeping past standard — server manages the table's expectations *before* they ask, buys time with a genuine touch, not an excuse. | Scenario cards: "Table 12's entrees are 12 minutes late, they have a movie at 7." Server practices the recovery line and the follow-through action. |
| **Fine Dining** | A course comes back wrong from the kitchen — the recovery has to be seamless enough that the guest never feels the kitchen made a mistake. Composure is the whole performance. | Mock service recovery under an audience: peers watch and score whether the recovery ever "broke character." |

### Module 3: THE HANDOFF *(Delegation & Accountability · Teamwork · Time Management)*

| Tier | What "The Handoff" Looks Like | How It's Taught |
|---|---|---|
| **QSR / Food Court** | Lead trusts a cashier to own register accuracy and cash handling without checking every drawer count — but reviews the numbers daily and coaches immediately on misses. | Shift leads run a "trust ladder": new hires start with double-checks, graduate to solo accountability once they hit 3 clean shifts in a row. |
| **Casual Dining** | Server and busser coordinate table turns without a manager quarterbacking every reset — clear ownership of who resets what, by when. | Team relay drills during slow hours: full table reset timed and scored as a two-person unit, not individually. |
| **Fine Dining** | Captain delegates full course choreography to a server team, stepping back to observe — intervening only if timing or standard slips, not to micromanage every plate. | "Captain's Eye" shadow shifts: a senior server runs the section solo while a captain observes silently and debriefs after, not during. |

### Module 4: THE MESSAGE *(Cross-Functional Communication · Conflict Resolution · Coaching & Developing Others)*

| Tier | What "The Message" Looks Like | How It's Taught |
|---|---|---|
| **QSR / Food Court** | Cashier flags a recurring complaint pattern (e.g., a menu item guests keep misunderstanding) up to the lead in one clear sentence during a lull — not buried in a vague "people keep asking about that thing." | "One-Sentence Reports": cashiers practice compressing a floor observation into a single, actionable line for their lead. |
| **Casual Dining** | Server and kitchen communicate a modification or allergy clearly enough that it survives being relayed twice before it hits the line. | Telephone-game drills with real ticket modifications — measuring what gets lost between server, expo, and line. |
| **Fine Dining** | Captain translates a VIP guest's unspoken preference into precise, private instruction to the entire team before the guest even sits down. | Pre-shift briefing simulations: captain must brief a full section on a VIP in under 90 seconds with zero ambiguity. |

## 3. SKILLS ASSESSMENT & ARCHETYPES

A descriptive rubric, not a 1-5 grading scale — nobody grows from a number. This describes *behavior on the floor* at four levels for each of the 5 Pillars, and the combination of the two strongest pillars reveals a **Hospitality Management Archetype**.

### The Rubric (per Pillar)

| Level | Emotional Intelligence | Decisiveness Under Pressure | Delegation & Accountability | Coaching & Developing | Cross-Functional Communication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Emerging** | Notices problems only after a guest complains out loud | Freezes or asks a manager before acting on routine calls | Does the task themselves rather than hand it off | Corrects mistakes but doesn't explain the "why" | Reports up in vague, reactive terms ("things are crazy") |
| **Developing** | Catches most guest cues but misses subtler staff-side signals | Makes calls, but second-guesses them out loud in front of the team | Delegates tasks but still checks every 10 minutes | Gives feedback, but only during scheduled reviews | Reports facts, but without the "so what" upper management needs |
| **Skilled** | Reads guest *and* staff dynamics in real time, adjusts approach on the fly | Makes fast calls and explains the reasoning briefly, without hesitation | Delegates outcomes, not just tasks, and checks results not process | Coaches in the moment, ties feedback to a specific, repeatable action | Translates floor chaos into a clear ask or update, unprompted |
| **Mastery** | Anticipates dynamics before they surface; teaches others to read the room | Owns high-stakes calls calmly enough that the team doesn't feel the pressure | Builds leads who delegate the same way — the trust ladder replicates itself | Turns every shift into a development opportunity without slowing service | Is the translation layer upper management relies on without having to ask |

### The Archetypes

- **The High-Volume Catalyst** *(Decisiveness + Emotional Intelligence)* — Thrives where speed and chaos collide. Turns a slammed food court into a well-oiled machine by making calls fast and reading the line's mood in real time. Best deployed where volume, not nuance, is the daily test.

- **The Floor Choreographer** *(Delegation & Accountability + Emotional Intelligence)* — Builds a team that runs like a rehearsed dance without needing constant direction. Excels in fine dining and casual dining floors where coordination and anticipation matter more than raw speed.

- **The Guest Experience Architect** *(Emotional Intelligence + Cross-Functional Communication)* — Designs the *feel* of a shift, not just the transactions. Reads the guest, then makes sure the whole team — and upper management — understands what that guest needed and why.

- **The Bridge Builder** *(Cross-Functional Communication + Coaching & Developing Others)* — The translator between floor and office. Naturally turns strategy into floor-level action and floor reality into upstream insight — the manager other managers go to when something needs explaining both ways.

- **The Talent Grower** *(Coaching & Developing Others + Delegation & Accountability)* — Builds people, not just shifts. Their outlets consistently produce the next generation of leads because they hand off real ownership early and coach relentlessly along the way.

*Delivery note:* Have participants self-score against the rubric, then have their direct supervisor score them independently. The gap between the two scores is often the most useful coaching conversation in the whole program.

```

SITUATION (2-3 sentences, specific, floor-real):
"You're mid-rush at [outlet]. [Specific trigger: 86'd item / upset
guest / short-staffed / POS issue / conflicting instructions].
[One added pressure detail: time constraint, VIP, understaffing]."

WHAT DO YOU DO?
  A) [Reactive/avoidant option — flags a real gap if chosen]
  B) [Technically correct but socially clumsy option]
  C) [Best-practice answer — ties directly to one of the 5 Pillars
      or Top 10 Soft Skills]
  D) [Overcorrection — technically "extra effort" but breaks
      standard/timing]

CORRECT ANSWER: [Letter]
SKILL BEING TESTED: [Name the Pillar or Soft Skill]
COACHING NOTE (shown after answering, regardless of choice):
  "Here's why C works on the floor: [1-2 sentences, real language,

   for [specific floor tell] next shift."
```

Build a bank of at least 10 scenarios per tier per module (120 total) so no two quiz sessions feel repeated.

## THE TOP 10 CORE HOSPITALITY SOFT SKILLS (Reference List)

1. **Active Listening** — Hearing the actual request, not the assumed one
2. **Empathy** — Feeling the guest's moment, not just processing their order
3. **Adaptability** — Shifting plans mid-shift without losing composure
4. **Conflict Resolution** — De-escalating without surrendering the standard
5. **Time Management** — Reading the clock without rushing the guest
6. **Stress Tolerance** — Staying steady when the floor isn't
7. **Teamwork** — Covering a teammate without being asked
8. **Clear Communication** — Saying the true thing in the fewest words
9. **Problem-Solving** — Finding the workaround before escalating
10. **Attention to Detail** — Catching the small thing before it becomes the big complaint

## THE 3 TEAM NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **The First Look** — Every guest gets genuine acknowledgment within seconds of arriving, no exceptions for how busy it is.
2. **Own It, Don't Toss It** — Every issue gets owned by whoever hears it first, until it's properly handed off — never "not my department."
3. **The Send-Off With Substance** — Every guest leaves with a specific, non-generic close tied to what they actually ordered or experienced.

## THE ART OF THE PRE-SHIFT

If the sections above are the system, this is the *engine start* — the five minutes before doors open that decide whether tonight's shift runs on autopilot or on purpose. A pre-shift isn't a briefing. It's a temperature check, a rehearsal, and a culture download, all in under five minutes.

### The 4 Elements

**1. FOCUS — One Big Idea, Not Nine**
86'd items and specials are maintenance, not leadership. Cover them in 30 seconds flat — that's what spot-checks throughout the shift are for. The real job of a pre-shift is picking **one single hospitality idea** and teaching it like it's the only thing that matters tonight, because for the next 8 hours, it is. A lead who repeats "eye contact within the first 5 seconds" every day for a week builds a habit. A lead who mentions nine different tips once each builds nothing — the team forgets all nine by lunch.

**2. CLARITY — Show the Behavior, Don't Name the Value**
"Be more hospitable" is a vague ask that gets a vague result — nobody knows what to *do* differently. "Walk the guest to the table instead of pointing" is a clear ask that gets a specific, repeatable action. Every Big Idea needs to be translated into one physical, observable behavior before it leaves the pre-shift huddle. If it can't be demonstrated in five seconds, it's not clear enough yet.

**3. ENERGY — They Catch Your Temperature Before Your Words**
The team reads a leader's energy in the first three seconds, before a single sentence is spoken. If a leader wouldn't want to be a guest listening to their own pre-shift, don't expect the team to bring energy to the floor after hearing it. This isn't about being loud — it's about being *present*. Flat delivery of a great idea still lands flat.

**4. INTERACTIVE — Talk *With* Them, Not *At* Them**
Stop lecturing. Ask the questions instead of answering them: *"Can someone repeat back what we just covered?" "What's an example of that from last night?" "Who had a win yesterday — tell us about it."* People remember what they say out loud far longer than what they hear said to them. Once the team knows they might get called on, they lean in instead of checking out — engagement becomes the expectation, not the exception.

### The One Big Idea Library

A rotating bank tied directly to the Top 10 Soft Skills and the 3 Non-Negotiables — one idea per day, repeated for a full week before rotating:

| Week Focus | The Big Idea | The Clear Action |
|---|---|---|
| Active Listening | "Repeat it back before you ring it." | Cashier repeats the order back in their own words before hitting total |
| The First Look | "Eyes up before words out." | Acknowledge the guest with eye contact before saying anything else |
| Own It, Don't Toss It | "If you hear it, you own it." | Whoever hears a complaint stays with the guest until it's resolved or properly handed off |
| Empathy | "Guess their day before you guess their order." | Notice one visible cue (rushed, tired, celebrating) and adjust tone accordingly |
| The Send-Off | "Name the item, not just 'enjoy.'" | Close every transaction referencing the specific item(s) purchased |

### 5-Minute Pre-Shift Script Shape

```
0:00-0:30  ENERGY CHECK-IN
           "Give me one word for how you're walking in today."
           (Quick around-the-room — sets tone, takes temperature)

0:30-1:00  WIN OF THE DAY
           "Who had a win yesterday? Tell us what happened."
           (Let them tell it — don't summarize it for them)

1:00-2:30  THE ONE BIG IDEA
           State it once, clearly, with the physical action.
           Demonstrate it, don't just describe it.

2:30-3:30  MAKE IT INTERACTIVE
           "Can someone repeat that back?"
           "Give me an example of when this comes up today."

3:30-4:00  86's / SPECIALS
           Fast, flat, no elaboration.

4:00-5:00  SEND-OFF
           One line of energy to walk out on the floor with.
```

This keeps the maintenance stuff (86's, specials) exactly where it belongs — quick and secondary — while the hospitality idea gets the weight, the repetition, and the follow-through it needs to actually change behavior on the floor.
